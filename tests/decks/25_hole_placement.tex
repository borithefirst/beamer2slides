\begin{frame}{Formulas on wrapped lines}
  When we estimate a population quantity from data we often use the sample mean and,
  under mild conditions, the squared error $\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - \bar x)^2$ shrinks with more samples,
  while the bias $\hat{\theta}_n^{(k)} - \theta$ stays fixed unless the estimator is corrected.
  A last sentence ends with a formula $\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i^2$.

  \medskip
  Two in a row, the second one on the next line: $\bar x = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i x_i$ and then
  the variance $\hat\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum_i (x_i - \bar x)^2$ both appear here.
\end{frame}
